vShell privacy policy

Effective 1 November 2026

vShell is a desktop bar and widget app for Windows, made by Steven ("V"). It runs on your own PC. This policy explains what vShell does with information, with particular attention to the Mail plugin's Sign in with Google, which is what Google's rules ask us to describe.

The short version

  • vShell has no servers of its own. Nothing you do in it is sent to the developer, and nothing is collected for analytics or advertising.
  • Everything vShell stores lives on your PC, in your Windows user profile. Passwords and sign-in tokens are stored encrypted for your Windows account.
  • vShell only talks to the services you set it up to talk to: your mail server, your Home Assistant, your media server, and so on.

Sign in with Google (the Mail plugin)

The Mail plugin can watch a Gmail account for unread messages. If you choose Sign in with Google, vShell asks Google for permission to use IMAP with your account (Google's https://mail.google.com/ scope) and for your email address.

What vShell reads. For the folder you choose (normally the Inbox), vShell counts the unread messages and reads the sender, subject and date of the unread ones, so it can show the count on your bar, list the messages in a popup, and notify you when new ones arrive. It also reads the list of your folders when you pick which folder to watch. It never reads message bodies or attachments.

What vShell changes. Only what you ask it to, by clicking: it can mark a message as read, and move a message to the Trash (or delete it where the server has no Trash folder). It does not send mail, and it does not change anything on its own.

What is stored, and where. The sign-in token Google gives vShell is stored in vShell's settings file on your PC (%APPDATA%\vShell\settings.json), encrypted with Windows' data protection so only your Windows account can read it. Message senders and subjects are kept in memory while vShell runs; the ones that appeared in a notification stay in vShell's notification history on your PC until you clear it. Your email address is stored with the account's settings so vShell knows which account it is.

Where the data goes. Only between your PC and Google's own servers (Google's sign-in and token services, and imap.gmail.com). vShell does not send your Google data, or anything derived from it, to the developer or to any third party. There is no cloud component.

Limited Use. vShell's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In plain terms: the data is used only to show you your own mail in vShell, is never used for advertising, and is never sold or shared.

Taking the access away. You can stop it at any time, in any of these ways:

  • In vShell: Settings > Plugins > Mail > Sign out on the account, or remove the account, or remove the Mail plugin. The stored token is deleted.
  • At Google: myaccount.google.com/permissions > vShell > Remove access. vShell's token stops working at once.
  • Uninstalling vShell and deleting the %APPDATA%\vShell folder removes everything it stored.

Other mail accounts

An account set up with a password (an app password for Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, or a mail server of your own) works the same way: the password is stored encrypted on your PC and is sent only to the mail server you typed in.

The rest of vShell

  • Settings and history are kept in %APPDATA%\vShell on your PC, with daily backups in the same place.
  • Plugins connect to services you choose and set up yourself (Home Assistant, Jellyfin, Seerr, Proxmox, UniFi, Uptime Kuma and others). Each connects directly from your PC to that service with the address and key you gave it, and to nothing else.
  • Weather fetches forecasts from Open-Meteo for the place you set.
  • Updates: vShell checks its release page for a newer version and downloads it from there. That is an ordinary web request; no account or personal information goes with it.
  • The log (%APPDATA%\vShell\vshell.log) is written on your PC for troubleshooting and never sent anywhere unless you send it.

Children

vShell is not aimed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
